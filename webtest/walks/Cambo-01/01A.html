The Woodland Circular Walk - 2.7Km - Approx 1Hr 20Min


Some Notes for the Walkers

Introduction:

This walk circles through the length of the woodland area that runs through the centre of the Cambo Estate. It leaves the woodland only briefly to skirt past the walled garden.

The gravel path is clear and wide enough for a wheeled walking aid. There are some inclines, both down and up, mainly on the return section, where extra care is necessary, particularly on the downward inclines.

Follow this route:

Leave the Stables Visitor Centre and head south, crossing the driveway to take the path on the left past the car park. Continue on this woodland path heading towards the entrance to the estate. At the next junction, cross the driveway and take the track back along the northbound path with the Cambo Burn on the right. Follow the burn as it meanders through the valley, known by the old Scots words as the Cambo Den, before crossing over the bridge and continuing with the burn on the left. At the next junction, climb up towards the edge of the woodland and through the gate into the field. Follow the path along the side of the field, skirting the Walled Garden, before re-entering the woodland at the first gate and heading down along the pathway until the bridge over the burn. Cross this bridge and turn left to lead back down towards Cambo House. Turn left with the house on the right. Turn right at the entrance to the Walled Garden and follow the path back around to the Visitor Centre.

Interesting Features:

At the beginning of the walk, the features are typical woodland, with some interesting rock formations and tree root systems visible on the northbound section, particularly as the path meanders beside the Cambo Burn.

Walk Leader Notes

General Description:

This walk takes you from the Stables Visitor Centre heading south past the car park and along a flat woodland path towards the entrance to the estate. Crossing the main driveway, we head down a sloping path towards the Cambo Burn. Keeping the burn on our right we follow the path as it meanders through the Cambo Den before crossing over a bridge then taking the left fork and continuing with the burn on our left.

As you walk along this section keep a lookout for the badger sett on the opposite bank of the burn. Just before the next bridge, we take a sharp right, up a fairly steep incline, through a gate bringing us to the edge of the woodland.

We follow the path along the side of the field, passed the compost bins, and re-enter the woodland where we continue down the path until we reach a fence with a gate, through which we pass instead of remaining on the path following the burn.

The other side of the gate leads us to a bridge over the burn where we turn left in the direction of the stables. The walk ends after we skirt round the north of the Walled Garden, through the Prairie Garden and back around to the Visitor Centre.

Pathway Conditions:

The gravel path is clear and wide enough for a wheeled walking aid. There are some inclines, both down and up, mainly on the return section, where extra care is necessary, particularly on the downward inclines.

Weather Considerations:

In winter, the path may be slippery in places during frosty weather but will remain mostly passable.

Interesting Features:

At the beginning of the walk, the features are typical woodland, with some interesting rock formations and tree root systems visible on the northbound section, particularly through the Cambo Den.

Background Information

These walks have been developed by the Monday Walking Group which meets at the Cambo Estate each Monday morning at 9:30, weather permitting. The walks take approximately one hour and are between 2 and 3 kilometres in length.

The group walks in association with Walking Scotland, a charity whose purpose is to encourage walking by providing a framework for organised walking groups. The walk leaders have received appropriate training to ensure the walks are suitable and safe for all. Many of the walks are also suitable for people who walk with wheeled devices.

The Cambo Estate is managed by the Cambo Heritage Trust, and operated to maintain the historic house, grounds and associated buildings and gardens in environmentally sustainable format for all to enjoy.
