Cambo Cottages Walk - 2.59Km - 1Hr 20Min(approx)


Some Notes for the Walkers

Introduction:

This walk takes you from the Stables Visitor Centre heading south past the car park and along a flat woodland path towards the entrance to the estate where it takes a right turn and heads through woodland on the inside of the estate perimeter wall. There are a number of small boardwalks on this section where care should be taken, particularly if wet.

Walking through this part of the woodland there are fields to the right where roe deer can be seen crossing and grey herons are regular visitors, the nests of which are in the treetops above you.The gravel path is clear and wide enough for a wheeled walking aid. There are some inclines, both down and up, mainly on the return section, where extra care is necessary, particularly on the downward inclines.

Further along this path is an old Icehouse, previously used to store fresh meat, fish, fruit and ice throughout the year before mechanical refrigeration was invented. Beyond the icehouse the path undulates and narrows, due to the vegetation, where single file walking is required.

Near the end of this path, on the left-hand side, is a rope swing that was used by the legendary Annmarie Taggart on her Cambo Health Walks.

Leaving the woodland, through a gate, a right turn is made down past Cambo Farm to a T-junction where another right turn is made on to a hard-core surface with hedgerows on either side. Be aware that vehicles can use this to gain access to Cambo House.

Along this section are the tennis courts, that were originally a curling pond, alongside which is the Curling Hut where the curling stones were stored. The Curling Hut is open to view.

Continuing along, with Cambo House on the left, the road sweeps to the right and the walk ends under the chestnut tree at the entrance to the Stables Visitor Centre.

Pathway Conditions:

The gravel path at the start of the walk is clear and wide enough for a wheeled walking aid. Through the woodland, adjacent to the estate perimeter wall, the path has undulations and can be soft underfoot. Beyond the woodland, the path remains firm in most weathers.

Weather Considerations:

In winter, the path may be slippery in places during frosty weather but will remain mostly passable.

Interesting Features:

At the beginning of the walk, the features are typical woodland, with some interesting rock formations and tree root systems visible on the northbound section, particularly through the Cambo Den.

Background Information

These walks have been developed by the Monday Walking Group which meets at the Cambo Estate each Monday morning at 9:30, weather permitting. The walks take approximately one hour and are between 2 and 3 kilometres in length.

The group walks in association with Walking Scotland, a charity whose purpose is to encourage walking by providing a framework for organised walking groups. The walk leaders have received appropriate training to ensure the walks are suitable and safe for all. Many of the walks are also suitable for people who walk with wheeled devices.

The Cambo Estate is managed by the Cambo Heritage Trust, and operated to maintain the historic house, grounds and associated buildings and gardens in environmentally sustainable format for all to enjoy.
