About this walk

Starting at the Stables Visitor Centre, the route heads out to the Cambo Mausoleum before continuing through fields and past the golf practice area. It then passes the estate storage compound and returns towards Cambo House, taking in the tennis courts and Curling Hut before finishing at the Stables.

This route offers a combination of estate history, open views and woodland or field paths. Keep to the route and take care around the golf practice area and working parts of the estate.

The walk

Description of the walk

This walk takes you from the Stables Visitor Centre heading south to the end of the car park then turn right onto a path made of large stone chips. Presently, this path is not suitable for any wheeled devices.

Approximately half-way along this path, the Mausoleum is on the left-hand side. Access can be gained most of the time unless a special event is taking place. The Mausoleum was built in 1821 as the burial chamber for the Erskine family. It is a stone-slabbed-vaulted chamber flanked by columbaria, ashlar front with arched entrance and pediment.

Further along this path there are fields on both sides where roe deer can be seen crossing and grey herons are regular visitors. At the crossroads turn right along a hard-packed earth path then left at the T-junction and right at a crossroads on to a tarmac road taking you (quietly!) past the golf practice area.

Just before the Clubhouse, turn right and, with the golf course on the left, head towards an Estate storage compound. Care should be taken passing through this area as it contains items of machinery, logs, pallets etc.

After this compound turn right in the direction of Cambo House and follow the path keeping Cambo House on the left. Arriving at a T-junction, directly opposite are the tennis courts, that were originally a curling pond, alongside which is the Curling Hut where the curling stones were stored. The Curling Hut is open to view.

Turn left and with Cambo House on the left, the road sweeps to the right and the walk finishes under the chestnut tree at the entrance to the Stables Visitor Centre.

Take care

Safety considerations

The initial stone-chip path is not suitable for wheeled devices. Take care passing through the estate storage compound, which contains machinery, logs and pallets. Keep quiet when passing the golf practice area.

Underfoot

Pathway conditions

The chipped path near the start, although wide enough, is not suitable for a wheeled walking aid. Throughout the remainder of the walk the path remains firm in most weathers.

Plan ahead

Weather considerations

Depending on weather conditions, paths may be slippery in places.

About our Walks

These walks have been developed by the Monday Walking Group which meets at the Cambo Stables each Monday morning at 9.30am, weather permitting.

The group walks are in association with Walking Scotland, a National Walking and Wheeling charity, whose purpose is to encourage walking by providing a framework for organised walking groups.

Walking Scotland use both terms walking and wheeling as it’s more inclusive. Both words represent the action of moving at a pedestrian’s pace, whether or not someone is unaided or using some kind of wheeled mobility aid.

The Walk Leaders have received appropriate training to ensure the walks are suitable and risk assessed for all attending, including those who use wheeled devices.

The walks are short, safe and social at a pace that we are able to talk. They last approximately 1 to 1¼ hours and between 1 and 5km in length. Depending on weather conditions, the paths may be slippery in places.

Cambo Gardens are managed by the Cambo Heritage Trust with its’ simple aim for Cambo to be a place ‘where people and plants grow’.

About Cambo Estate

The Cambo Estate has been in the Erskine family for 300 years and in 1976 Lady Catherine Erskine and her husband Sir Peter took over the estate and began offering volunteering and training opportunities to the local community and opened to the public in the 1980s at snowdrop time when the grounds around the house are carpeted white.

Developing the walled garden was a logical next step in making a garden of interest to visitors all through the year. The head gardener introduced bold planting inspired by the New Perennial Movement* using grasses and late season perennials that didn’t need staking or deadheading, achieving high output for low input. In late summer and autumn these plantings reach a peak of beauty and interest as the grasses flower amongst herbaceous perennials including persicaria, asters, eupatorium and sedums.

The garden has entered a new chapter which may include training opportunities, an event space, and a contemporary cutting garden.

*Note: a naturalistic landscape and horticultural design style that mimics wild plant communities like prairies, meadows, and steppes.
